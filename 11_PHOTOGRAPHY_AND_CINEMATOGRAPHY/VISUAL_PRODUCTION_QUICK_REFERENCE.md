## 📹 PART 3: CAMERA, LENSES, LIGHTING & CONTINUITY

### 3.1 Power Dynamics of Camera Angles [Source-Backed Facts]
*   **Low Angle / Worm's-Eye View:** Camera below eye-line pointing upward along Y-axis [274]. Transfers absolute power, dominance, and monumental scale to the subject [228, 274]. Pair with a slow orbiting or arc shot for heroic status [274].
*   **High Angle / Bird's-Eye View:** Camera above subject pointing downward [273]. Inject vulnerability, smallness, or submission [228, 273]. A 90° extreme vertical overhead shot conceals depth, creating suspense [273].
*   **Eye Level:** Camera level with subject's eyes [275]. Horizon line in the center [275]. Represents neutral, honest documentary realism [275].
*   **Dutch / Oblique Angle:** Camera physically tilted on horizontal axis (Z-axis roll) to trigger cognitive unease [275].
    *   *Oblique Constraints:* Must have clear horizontal/vertical reference lines in background [275]. The tilt must be at least 15 degrees to look highly intentional [275].

### 3.2 Lens Physics & Spatial Geometry [Source-Backed Facts]
*   **Ultra-Wide (10–24mm):** Exaggerates foreground/background distance, introducing barrel distortion [228, 289]. Used for scale or action-cam aesthetics [228].
*   **Standard (35–50mm):** Mimics human eye vision for honest perspective and documentary realism [228, 289].
*   **Portrait (50–85mm):** Gentle facial compression and shallow depth of field for polish and intimacy [228].
*   **Telephoto (85–200mm+):** Extreme spatial compression, pushing foreground and background tightly together, making far-away backgrounds look massive and immediate [289].

### 3.3 Camera Movement & Optical Formulas [Source-Backed Facts]
*   **Optical Zoom vs. Physical Dolly:**
    *   *Optical Zoom:* Static camera; internal glass shifts to magnify [290]. Feels unnatural; only used slowly for unease [290] or as a rapid "Crash Zoom" [290].
    *   *Physical Dolly:* Camera moves through 3D space on the Z-axis [290]. Dynamically alters perspective and spatial relationships organically [290].
*   **The Dolly Zoom (Vertigo Effect) Formulas:**
    *   *Formula A (Dolly In + Zoom Out):* Foreground subject remains constant scale; background expands, stretches, and falls away [291]. Signals panic, shock, or realization [291].
    *   *Formula B (Dolly Out + Zoom In):* Foreground subject remains constant scale; background compresses and pulls forward [292]. Signals extreme tunnel vision or claustrophobia [292].
*   **Shutter & setup geometry:**
    *   *The 180-Degree Shutter Rule:* Lock shutter speed at exactly double the frame rate ($Shutter\ Speed = \frac{1}{2 \times FPS}$) to capture natural motion blur [288]. Critical for Whip Pans [288].
    *   *The 30-Degree Rule:* When cutting between two cameras capturing the same subject, the second camera's optical axis must be offset by at least 30° [293]. Angle cuts $<30°$ trigger distracting, glitchy jump cuts [293].
