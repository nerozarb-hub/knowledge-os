## 🧠 SECTION 1: SOURCE-BACKED FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES
Direct-response success requires separating long-term strategic positioning from temporary, executable platform hacks [9, 357]. The following core operating principles represent the physical laws of modern performance marketing:

### 1. "The Creative IS the Targeting"
Post-iOS 14, platform delivery algorithms have less native demographic and interest-based tracking data [82, 104, 121, 400]. Under Meta's **Andromeda Architecture**, you do not select interests, lookalikes, or custom retargeting lists [6, 45, 121, 356, 381, 412]. The words in your transcript, the objects in your video frames, and the text in your static ad overlays are read by Meta's NLP/Computer Vision models to find and match your customers [6, 45, 356, 381]. 

When you display bold, legible text overlay identifying a specific pain point or persona (e.g., *"Tailored fits for athletic builds"*), Meta's **Rosetta Subsystem** extracts this text via Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and passes it to visual classifiers [45, 48, 381, 383]. Andromeda maps this semantic data directly to users showing behaviors, scroll speeds, and content interactions matching that profile [45, 48, 381, 383]. Your ad acts as the code that programs the delivery engine [48, 383].

### 2. Volume Negates Luck & The $1-to-$2 Machine Paradigm
Success in customer acquisition is a game of probability, not creative inspiration [80, 82, 103, 400, 412]. High input volume guarantees learning speed and reduces performance volatility [80, 82, 400]. Facebook advertising must be approached as a pure mathematical feedback loop—the objective is to consistently put **$1 in and generate $2 out** [101, 103, 412]. If the machine is fed high-converting, conceptually diverse assets paired with consolidated, broad platform settings, Meta is incentivized to optimize performance and protect your budget to keep you spending [101, 103, 412]. 

### 3. Create Flow, Monetize Flow, Then Add Friction
Operators must run "water" through the business machine to find where it breaks [80, 85, 400, 407]. You must generate raw volume (flow) first, monetize it, and only then add friction (such as inserting video sales letters, adding qualifying form fields, charging entry fees, or tightening targeting) to optimize the funnel [80, 85, 93, 400, 407]. 

### 4. Optimize Front-to-Back, Build Back-to-Front
Allocate 80% of creative energy to the first 5 seconds (the hook) because it frames the user's entire experience and governs downstream Click-Through Rates [82, 400]. In 2025/2026, the modern customer hook rate decision window has compressed to **1 second** [5, 355]. Conversely, build your business back-to-front: high back-end lifetime gross profit (LTV-GP) funds front-end acquisition, allowing you to outbid your competition in the attention auction and establish an ethical monopoly [80, 82, 400].

### 5. Proof Over Promise
Your promise is never unique; every competitor claims they can solve the same customer problem [82, 400]. What is unique is your proof—the specific, difficult-to-fake, verified outcomes and case studies you deliver [82, 400].

### 6. The Hook is a Filter
The hook does not exist to entertain everyone [6, 281, 356]. It exists to visually and audibly capture the attention of a highly specific target demographic (the *self-selection* mechanic), weeding out unqualified scrollers to preserve conversion-rate efficiency and avoid polluting the pixel's training pool [6, 27, 356].

### 7. The Squint Test for Creative Diversity
Meta's Andromeda uses similarity clustering to group ads that look, sound, or read similarly into single **"Entity IDs"** to manage its computational load [50, 384]. If you lay out your top active ads in a slide deck and squint your eyes, they must not look identical in color, format, or layout [6, 86, 356]. If they do, Andromeda clusters them, throttles budget distribution to a single unit, and fatigues the account [6, 74, 105, 340, 356]. 

### 8. Format-Intent Alignment
The visual format must match the customer's funnel intent [6, 356]. Organic-style UGC and street interviews educate top-of-funnel (TOF) cold audiences; mini-VSLs and product teardowns persuade middle-of-funnel (MOF) problem-aware targets; high-contrast static offers, testimonials, and scarcity blocks close bottom-of-funnel (BOFU) retargeted warm leads [6, 143, 257, 259, 356, 434, 477].

### I. The 4-Lever Creative Diversity Matrix
To bypass Andromeda's similarity clumping (which throttles spend to a single ad unit and drives up CPMs), creative production must diversify across four distinct levers [9, 12, 50, 359, 384]. **At least three of these four levers must change entirely** to register as a novel Entity ID in Meta's database [12, 359]:
1. **Persona**: The targeted consumer avatar (e.g., pregnant moms needing morning sickness solutions vs. busy executives with migraines) [147, 435].
2. **Messaging**: The psychological angle or "Bleeding Neck" pain point addressed (e.g., price objection vs. convenience vs. chemical-free health) [20, 203, 367].
3. **Hook**: The first 1–3 seconds of visual, verbal, and textual pattern interrupts designed to stop the scroll [10, 450].
4. **Format**: The visual wrapper (e.g., unpolished talking-head UGC, high-fi founder stories, B2B whiteboard workflows, text-heavy "breakup letters," or "boring" Dynamic Product Ads with custom overlays) [4, 9, 10, 354, 357].

### II. The Problem/Solution Umbrella Framework
The core psychological sequence for direct-response sales [11, 358]:
1. **The Hook (0–3s)**: Call out the specific target demographic or paint point using extreme visual scenarios or stark "I-statements" [11, 358, 364].
2. **The Agitation (3–15s)**: Aggregate the pain point. Isolate a highly granular, emotional friction point and mimic the customer's exact internal dialogue [11, 35, 358, 375].
3. **The Solution (15–25s)**: Introduce the product cleanly as the only viable escape path from the agitated reality [11, 358].
4. **The Objection Crushing (25–40s)**: Raise and destroy the customer's biggest purchase barrier natively during a product demonstration (e.g., showing a front-clasp bra and physically pulling it to prove it won't pop open on camera) [11, 358, 364, 372].
5. **The Proof (40–50s)**: Layer in hard, quantified facts, Trustpilot star ratings, clinical studies, or before-and-after visual evidence [11, 82, 358, 364].
6. **The CTA (50–60s)**: Deliver a strict, direct instruction on how to purchase, clearly stating the offer and setting expectations for the landing page destination [11, 17, 358].

### III. The Hero & Villain Framework
Designed to structure aggressive comparative scripts [142, 440]:
1. **The Villain**: Introduce an industry standard, a competitor, or a toxic chemical compound as the villain (e.g., standard plastic razors that clog and harbor bacteria [142]; chemical-laden drugstore soap bars [142, 440]).
2. **The Aggravation**: Twist the knife [142, 440]. Make the viewer feel the physical pain or discomfort of the villain (e.g., razor burn and ingesting microplastics) [142].
3. **The Hero**: Introduce your product as the obvious, natural savior (e.g., Leaf Thorn razor with cents-per-blade refills [142]; underbrush gum using natural tree sap [142]).
4. **The Proof**: Shoot a raw product demonstration or visual comparison highlighting superior efficacy [142].
5. **The CTA**: Deliver a direct response call-to-action outlining a risk-reversed offer [142, 176].

### IV. The Contrast Principle (Us vs. Them Grids)
A highly visual framework commonly deployed on static ads and carousel slides [143, 434]:
* **"Them" Column (Alternative/Disposable)**: Marked in a desaturated, cluttered style. Copy features negative, friction-inducing terms: *"Toxic," "Inferior," "Fills Landfills," "Chemically Loaded," "Boring Retainers."* [143, 210, 434]
* **"Us" Column (Brand/System)**: Highlighted using high-contrast, premium Visual Brutalism (Void Black backdrop, olive radial glow, bold typography) [122, 134, 188, 325, 482]. Copy features high-status, system-first terms: *"Sustainable," "Clean," "Zero Retainers," "Deployed in 4 Days."* [122, 134, 143, 210, 331, 434, 482]

### V. Savannah Sanchez's 25 High-Converting Hook Frameworks
Culled from $100M+ in analyzed ad spend, these hooks prioritize pattern interruption and curiosity gaps to stop scrolls in under 0.5 seconds [46, 291, 382, 387]:
1. **Post-It Note Reveal**: Cover the product or a laptop screen with a handwritten question on a Post-It. Peel it off to show the visual answer [387].
2. **Sunglasses Text Reflection**: Zoom in on a key value proposition or a shocking chart reflected in a creator's sunglasses [387].
3. **Blurred Focus Technique**: Open with highly blurred text on paper, slowly bringing it into sharp focus over the first 1.5 seconds [387].
4. **Phone Screen Magic**: Animate text or a client dashboard appearing magically on a phone screen surrounded by the physical product [387].
5. **Rock, Paper, Scissors Decision**: Two creators play a game to choose between "saving money" vs. "buying the product" (the product always wins) [387].
6. **Heads or Tails Choice**: Flip a coin: Heads = Save money; Tails = Try this product [387].
7. **Remote Control Product Selection**: Use a TV remote in stop-motion to "click through" and display different product SKUs [387].
8. **Chase Sequence**: Absurd clip of a creator literally chasing after a delivery truck or the product on foot [387].
9. **Cloud Message Mystery**: Creator points to the sky saying, *"Pay attention to the message in the clouds,"* then holds up a small sign with a text benefit [387].
10. **Fake Text Exchange Discovery**: Creator receives a text asking about their secret, then records a native voice note replying [387].
11. **Window Stalking Scenario**: Creator looks out a window, spots someone outside using the product, and initiates a fake text exchange [387].
12. **Husband's Surprise Validation**: Off-camera partner walks up saying, *"I can't believe how good that tip you gave me was!"* [387].
13. **Photo Collage Opening**: Open with a static, influencer-style photo collage before transitioning to high-pacing video b-roll [387].
14. **Reverse Drop Effect**: Drop the product and reverse the clip so it "flies" back into the creator's hand [387].
15. **Jump Out of Phone**: Creator appears to jump physically out of a smartphone screen into real-life scene [387].
16. **Multi-Screen Self Conversation**: Creator has an edited, fast-paced conversation with themselves across three separate screens [387].
17. **TikTok Comment Skeptic**: Overlay a skeptical or negative competitor comment and have the creator respond to it directly [387].
18. **"People Always Ask Me" Format**: *"People always ask me, Sarah, how do you fix [problem]? Well, my name's not Sarah, but I do know how [product] works!"* [387].
19. **Narrator Voice Over**: Use a dramatic, AI-generated voiceover (e.g., ElevenLabs) introducing the creator and their specific struggle [296, 387, 461].
20. **Megaphone Announcements**: Use a physical megaphone to announce a flash sale or PSA [387].
21. **Whiteboard Explainers**: Hand-drawn visual graphs, flows, or lists drawn in real-time on a physical whiteboard [387].

23. **Man on the Street Interviews**: Coordinate two creators to film a "spontaneous" street Q&A regarding a specific pain point [387].
24. **Story Time Journal**: Write a personal discovery story in a physical journal, highlighting the brand with a colored marker [387].
25. **Multi-Creator Mashup**: Rapid-fire cuts showing 5+ diverse creators holding or using the product in under 3 seconds [387].

### VI. The Before-After-Bridge (B.A.B.) Protocol
An ultra-high-converting copywriting framework optimized for educational carousels and static messaging [12, 135, 489]:
* **Before**: Agitate the reader's current painful reality with exact, raw customer language. *E.g., "You spend 40 hours a week on Canva, and your phone never rings."* [12, 135, 489]
* **After**: Paint a clear, realistic picture of the desired, automated state. *E.g., "A silent, high-end content system that books three qualified client calls every Monday."* [12, 135, 489]
* **Bridge**: Position your proprietary infrastructure as the only logical mechanism to cross the gap. *E.g., "We install the NERO Sprint. We do it in 4 days."* [12, 135, 136, 489]

### VII. The 3:2:2 Dynamic Creative Protocol
Designed by Charley Tichenor IV, this framework acts as a structured multivariate test inside a single ad unit, allowing the algorithm to find the "Efficiency Zone" where the strongest psychological trigger meets the most efficient delivery pattern [52, 274, 386]:
* **3 Visual Assets**: Three distinct images or short video hooks (e.g., Hook A: Problem-driven; Hook B: ASMR/Visual; Hook C: Testimonial) [52, 274, 386].
* **2 Primary Texts**: Two divergent copy angles (e.g., Text A: Short benefit-focused, <125 characters; Text B: Medium storytelling/social proof narrative) [52, 274, 289, 386].
* **2 Headlines**: Two benefit-driven, high-contrast headlines [52, 274, 386].
* **Implementation Rule**: Toggle "Dynamic Creative" at the ad set level for lead campaigns, or "Flexible Format" at the ad level for sales campaigns [46, 52, 128, 382, 386].

### VIII. The PMS (Pain, Mindset, Solution) Framework
The operational bridge linking organic internet humor directly to measurable pipeline revenue [31, 35, 39, 40]:
1. **Pain (The Anchor)**: Isolate a highly granular, emotional friction point experienced by your ideal customer profile (ICP). Avoid generic platitudes; mimic their exact internal dialogue [31, 35, 39, 40].
2. **Mindset (The Match)**: Map that specific pain point to a currently trending meme template or vertical video format that naturally represents that exact emotional state (e.g., panic, realization, exhaustion) [31, 35, 39, 40].
3. **Solution (The Bridge)**: Maintain the visual asset as 100% entertainment to protect engagement metrics [25, 31, 33]. Position your service as the logical, frictionless next step entirely within the caption, pinned comment, or profile link [23, 31, 35].

### SOP 1: Customer Insight & Review Mining (Deep Empathy Research Protocol)
* **Objective**: Extract raw, un-sanitized customer language and key psychological purchase drivers to build the core script library [12, 90, 118, 245, 359].

  2. Open a clean, dedicated LLM instance (Claude Sonnet preferred) [12, 407, 359]. Upload the reviews CSV along with any brand guidelines and competitor product URLs [12, 125, 359, 435].

  5. Identify "grainy textures," "bad smells," or "broken support" in rival brands to turn them into an "objection-led" hook [435]. Save this compiled document to `/workspace/scratch/` as the **Brand Brain** [12, 124, 126, 359].

     * Create a dedicated Campaign named `[CHALLENGER] Advantage_Sales_Champion_Ad_Sets` [55, 269, 389].

### SOP 4: Server-Side Signal Engineering (Pixel-to-CAPI Redundancy)
* **Objective**: Feed Meta's ad delivery engine high-fidelity, adblock-proof conversion signals to maximize Event Match Quality (EMQ) and increase ROAS by up to 22% [56, 204, 207, 289].
* **Step-by-Step Execution**:
  1. **Deploy Conversions API (CAPI)**: Connect your storefront or CRM (such as Shopify, custom node backends, or Popsixle/wetracked.io) directly to Meta's servers [56, 150, 205, 245, 289].
  2. **Set Up Deduplication**: Ensure your browser Pixel and server CAPI fire identical event names (e.g., `Purchase`, `Lead`, `AddToCart`) [56, 205]. Configure your tracking scripts to generate a completely unique, matching `event_id` string for both client-side and server-side payloads of the same event [56, 205].
  3. **Enrich Customer Match Parameters**: Instruct your server-side payload to securely hash and pass all available first-party data parameters [56, 206]. Enforce Hashing (**SHA-256**) for: *email, phone, external_id, client_ip_address, client_user_agent, fbp, fbc, zip, city, and country* [56, 206].
  4. **Audit Match Metrics**: Navigate to Meta Events Manager -> Integration tab. Verify that your **Event Match Quality (EMQ) Score is > 8.0/10** [56, 290]. If the score is lower, audit your backend database query to ensure all customer variables are successfully passing through CAPI [56, 206].

### SOP 5: Click-to-WhatsApp (CTWA) Conversational Funnels
* **Objective**: Bypass slow, high-friction landing pages by driving cold traffic directly into automated, qualified lead-generation chats on WhatsApp [57, 103, 105, 108, 116, 288].
* **Step-by-Step Execution**:
  1. **Account Onboarding**: Link your official WhatsApp Business number to your Meta Business Manager and complete company GST/PAN verification [57, 110, 482].
  2. **Campaign Creation**: Open Ads Manager, select the **Leads** or **Engagement** objective [57, 110].

## 🚫 SECTION 8: FAILURE MODES, ANTI-PATTERNS & PITFALLS
Operators must actively audit accounts to eliminate these common performance-killing traps:

### 1. The "Melon Explosion" Trap (Clickbait Hook Decoupling)
* **The Defect**: Using a wild, highly engaging visual hook that has absolutely nothing to do with the product (e.g., showing a watermelon exploding to sell skincare) [18, 151, 365, 444].
* **The Cost**: This gets a massive 70% Hook Rate but zero conversions because it attracts unqualified traffic, suffers immediate site bounces, and destroys the algorithm's intent matching model [18, 151, 365, 444].
* **The Mitigation**: Ensure your scroll stopper is always **conceptually relevant** to the product's pain point (e.g., crushing feet into flaky croissants to illustrate dry, flaky heels) [151, 173, 174, 444].

### 2. "Lazy Volume" (Minor Variant Fatigue)
* **The Defect**: Churning out 50 ad units with the exact same video body and just changing one word in the text overlay [3, 18, 73, 106, 353, 365].
* **The Cost**: Meta's computer vision groups these under a single similarity score, throttling spend to a single unit and fatiguing the account [3, 18, 74, 105, 340, 353, 365].
* **The Mitigation**: Enforce profound **conceptual diversity**—testing distinct personas, messages, hooks, and formats in structured testing campaigns [3, 18, 138, 353, 365].

### 3. Fully Baked AI Assets
* **The Defect**: Generating static ads entirely inside image models (e.g., Midjourney) where the copy and logos are rendered as flat, un-editable pixels [18, 365, 445].
* **The Cost**: This creates an operational nightmare; you cannot make rapid messaging adjustments, fix spelling errors, or translate languages without rebuilding the entire asset [18, 124, 458, 559, 365].
* **The Mitigation**: Generate clean background plates plate-by-plate and use **Canva Magic Layers** to automatically separate elements and overlay editable, clean vector typography [13, 18, 465, 436].

### 4. Superfluous "Brandy" Clutter
* **The Defect**: Over-designing a static ad with heavy brand logos, pretty borders, and corporate messaging (the "burrow" couch style) [18, 365].
* **The Cost**: This triggers immediate consumer ad blindness and fails on native vertical feeds [18, 391, 477, 365].
* **The Mitigation**: Make "ugly" ads that fit organically into the consumer's social feeds (e.g., raw unedited phone footage, green screen talking heads, fake tweets) [121, 140, 194, 520, 898].

### 5. UX Native Scam Formats
* **The Defect**: Running "fake" notification ads, fake missed calls, or ungrounded warning screens (e.g., fake airdrop overlays) [18, 365].
* **The Cost**: While they capture top-line attention, they generate a terrible 0.2% click-through rate because they annoy the customer and confuse the algorithm's targeting model [18, 941, 942, 998, 365].
* **The Mitigation**: Ground all visual hooks in real, physically believable value and logical pain points [18, 365].

### 6. The "Existing Customer" DPA Trap
* **The Defect**: Running catalog ads (DPAs) on broad targeting without realizing they are actually converting existing, warm brand buyers [18, 365].
* **The Cost**: The attributed in-platform ROAS appears highly profitable, but the incremental new customer CPA is terrible once brand-excluder lift tests are applied [18, 190, 998, 365].
* **The Mitigation**: Upload historical buyer lists strictly as custom exclusions to isolate cold acquisition [83, 125, 402].

### 7. The PayPal Account Warming Trap
* **The Defect**: Linking PayPal as the primary payment method for new Meta ad accounts [86, 94, 115, 118, 410, 418].
* **The Cost**: PayPal regularly triggers automated account flag verification blocks, leading to immediate account bans [86, 94, 115, 118, 410, 418].
* **The Mitigation**: Use direct corporate credit cards to minimize platform risk and capture cash-back/points leverage [86, 94, 115, 118, 410, 418].

### 8. The 3-Strike Credit Card Fraud Lock
* **The Defect**: Entering credit card details incorrectly three consecutive times during the initial ad account setup [94, 115, 118, 410, 418].
* **The Cost**: Meta's security scripts flag this as card-cloning activity, causing an automated, permanent security fraud lock on the Business Manager before ads can launch [94, 115, 118, 410, 418].
* **The Mitigation**: Carefully double-verify all billing addresses and payment numbers prior to submission [94, 115, 118, 410, 418].

### 1. Unrestricted Broad Targeting vs. 1–3M Sweet Spots [51, 67, 385, 397]
* **The Debate**: School A (Charley Tichenor, Disrupter School) argues that targeting is completely obsolete post-iOS 14, and that advertisers must go 100% broad (unrestricted country, age, gender) to give the algorithm maximum data liquidity [51, 67, 385]. School B (Giovanni Perilli) suggests that targeting should be bounded within "sweet spots" of 1 to 3 million users to maintain relevancy and prevent "burn to learn" capital drains [51, 67, 385].
* **The Resolution**: During early-stage testing or for ad accounts spending under $2,000/month, bounding target audiences within 1-3M users provides a baseline parameter to avoid rapid capital depletion [51, 67, 385]. However, once an account has sufficient historical pixel data, transitioning to unrestricted broad targeting unlocks the full predictive power of MTIA sequence learning [51, 67, 385].

### 2. Broad Bidding vs. Cost Caps & Cost Controls [51, 68, 385, 397]
* **The Debate**: Tichenor advocates for lowest-cost (broad) bidding, allowing the campaign to run completely free without manual bid limits to gather clean signal profiles [51, 68, 385]. Taylor Holiday (Common Thread Collective) argues this is highly risky, especially during competitive holiday seasons, and insists on using Cost Caps, Bid Caps, or tROAS to guarantee margin efficiency [51, 68, 385].
* **The Resolution**: Brand scale and cash reserves dictate the strategy [51, 68, 385]. High-budget e-commerce stores with deep pockets benefit from broad bidding to rapidly claim auction inventory and build massive similar-user cohorts [51, 68, 385]. Smaller brands or businesses with strict margin constraints must deploy Cost Caps to protect daily spend from holiday CPM spikes, acknowledging that caps may throttle delivery if creative scores are weak [51, 68, 385].

### 3. Rapid Creative Volume vs. Concepts over Ads [51, 69, 385, 395]
* **The Debate**: Gil Chaimovski (Meta Israel) states that ads die faster due to creative fatigue, and that top DTC brands must produce 50-70 new ads weekly [51, 69, 385, 395]. Conversely, Sam Piliero (The Moonlighters) warns that constantly creating new concepts is an agency myth; advertisers should find 2-3 winning concepts and "run them until the wheels fall off," iterating only on the winning templates [51, 69, 385, 395].
* **The Resolution**: Do not confuse *creative iteration* with *creative variation* [51, 69, 385, 395]. The optimal workflow is a **flywheel approach**: produce 2-3 major conceptual angles (e.g., Us vs. Them, Problem/Solution) [51, 69, 385], and then generate 5-10 high-velocity iterations (swapping the first 3-second hook, updating overlays, animating static elements) to prevent Andromeda's similarity clusters from throttling your reach [31, 51, 69, 385, 395].

### 4. Traditional Loss Aversion vs. Transactional Heuristics [239, 250, 515]
* **The Debate**: For decades, behavioral economics assumed that the WTA-WTP gap (the Endowment Effect) was driven by Loss Aversion—the emotional pain of losing an owned asset [250, 515]. 
* **The Resolution**: The Smitizsky, Liu, & Gneezy "Pay-to-Keep" (PTK) study proved that when owners are asked to spend money to keep an asset they already hold, their valuation matches their acquisition price (WTP), displaying no loss aversion [250, 515]. The WTA-WTP gap is actually driven by transactional market heuristics: buyers instinctively "bid low" and sellers instinctively "ask high" [250, 515]. Marketers must align offers around transaction heuristics rather than pure emotional panic [250, 515].

### 5. The AI Scriptwriting Contradiction [25, 370]
* **The Debate**: Fraser Cottrell famously boasts about writing six-figure direct-response ad scripts "without ever touching AI" [25, 174, 809, 370] and states that AI writes "horrible, generic slop" [25, 281, 295, 370]. However, he and other sources concurrently teach complete workflows using Claude to generate JSON image prompts [25, 459, 370], use AI voiceovers (ElevenLabs) to automate direct-response UGC mashups [25, 296, 461, 370], and sell courses with dedicated AI creative production modules [25, 173, 370].
* **The Resolution**: Pure text AI prompts generate generic, smoothed-out slop [18, 855]. AI must never write raw copywriting transcripts natively [18, 855]. However, AI is exceptionally powerful as a **structural and administrative tool** (generating visual layout specs, organizing reviews CSVs, and automating the mechanical editing pipeline) [13, 18, 465, 436].

### Gaps in the Sources (QA Gaps)

1. **Enterprise Procurement Constraints**: The sales and closing frameworks are heavily optimized for founder-led B2C e-commerce or small B2B agencies [268, 521]. They omit the complex procurement, legal, and security review cycles (SOC2, MSA, SOW) required to close strategic enterprise contracts [268, 521].
2. **Technical Google/Meta API Coding**: While the SOPs advocate connecting Conversions API (CAPI) and building no-code Opal Super Gems, they do not provide specific server-side code blocks, Cloudflare routing schemas, or API credit burn rates for high-volume setups [75, 289, 595].
3. **FTC Comparative Ad Compliance**: The scripting frameworks encourage taking minor competitor product flaws and exaggerating them "10 times worse" on camera (e.g., linking body wash to infertility) [25, 370]. There is zero guidance on how to navigate FTC false-advertising laws or brand cease-and-desist orders [28, 373].
