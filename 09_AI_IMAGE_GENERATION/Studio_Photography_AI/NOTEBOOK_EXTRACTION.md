*   **Rule:** Every quality, texture, or spatial relationship must be commanded using **precise physical parameters**.

## 3.2 The Structured Layer Method

## 3.3 The "Anti-Plastic" Material Shading Doctrine
The most immediate indicator of generative imagery is the "plastic sheen" caused by improper surface physics modeling. The NEROZARB system enforces the inclusion of micro-surface properties:

### 3.3.1 Subsurface Scattering (SSS)
Used for translucent materials like human skin, soap, wax, flower petals, leaves, and liquids.
*   **The Physics:** Light must penetrate the outer boundary (epidermis/surface), scatter multiple times at irregular angles within the material, and exit at a different point with wavelength-dependent absorption (e.g., red scatters furthest in human skin, creating a warm, flesh-like glow) [79, 174].
*   **The Prompting Terms:** "Burley Subsurface Scattering with a 1.2cm scatter radius," "Random Walk SSS model with red wavelength-deep internal scattering," "Translucent wax with a soft internal glow" [11, 156, 170, 194].

### 3.3.2 Vellus Hair & Asperity Scattering
Vellus hair ("peach fuzz") consists of short (<2mm), thin, light-colored translucent hairs covering the face and body [123, 325].
*   **The Physics:** In backlit scenarios, light striking these micro-fibers from behind undergoes **asperity scattering**, illuminating the silhouette and creating a soft, halo-like edge glow that separates the subject from the background [109, 194]. Without it, human skin looks like a cold 3D render or mannequin [29, 30].
*   **The Prompting Terms:** "Micro-pore texture with realistic vellus hair (peach fuzz) along the facial silhouette, illuminated by backlight to create asperity scattering," "Fine facial peach fuzz catching the rim light" [25, 95].

### 3.3.3 Anisotropic Highlights
Used for materials with directional micro-structures, such as brushed metals, hair, and woven fabrics [45, 100].
*   **The Physics:** Light reflections on anisotropic surfaces stretch perpendicularly to the direction of the micro-grooves (or strands), creating a linear or radial highlight pattern rather than a uniform circular reflection [79, 88].
*   **The Prompting Terms:** "Anisotropic reflections stretching across the brushed steel surface," "Marschner hair model showing distinct primary and secondary anisotropic highlights" [25, 79, 88, 96].

### 3.3.4 Worsted Wool Fiber & Weave DNA
High-end tailoring must be described with micro-geometry [195].
*   **The Physics:** Super worsted wool (e.g., Super 120s to 150s) consists of fibers between 16.25 and 17.75 microns in diameter, giving a tight, uniform weave that drapes elegantly and has a subtle, non-synthetic luster [234, 236].
*   **The Prompting Terms:** "Super 120s worsted wool (17.75-micron fibers) with a smooth twill weave, showing natural drape and a subtle, soft luster under diffused lighting," "Sharkskin basket-weave with two-tone diagonal contrast, showing realistic textile grain" [64, 101, 195].

## SOP 4: Prompt Contradiction Auditing & Error Recovery
To prevent AI model confusion and "mushed" rendering, run every prompt through this auditing workflow before execution [23]:
1.  **Scan for Adjective Pollution:** Remove words like "beautiful", "hyperrealistic", "stunning", "masterpiece".
2.  **Verify Lighting Geometry:** Ensure you have not requested conflicting lights. 
    *   *Audit:* If prompt says "soft overcast daylight" AND "dramatic studio strobe," it is invalid. Choose one.
3.  **Verify Optical Math:** Ensure focal length and aperture align with the composition.
    *   *Audit:* If prompt says "wide-angle 15mm landscape shot" with "extremely shallow f/1.2 background bokeh," this is an optical contradiction (wide-angle lenses have deep depth of field). Change to "85mm prime lens at f/1.4." [94, 187]
4.  **Error Recovery (On Fail):** If a render fails or contains artifacts:
    *   *Rule of 3:* **Do not iterate more than 3 times on the same image thread.** Over-iteration degrades shadow structures and bakes in noise.
    *   *Action:* If it fails on try 3, discard the thread. Break down the prompt back to Layer 1 (Subject only) in a brand-new thread, generate a clean base, and incrementally add one layer at a time.

# 7. Quality-Control Guardrails & Failure Modes

### 7.1 Key Failure Modes & Actionable Mitigations

#### 7.1.1 The "AI Plastic Sheen" (Waxy Epidermis)
*   **The Flaw:** Model defaults to smooth, airbrushed, cartoonish skin or plastic product bodies because of latent-space bias toward retouched social media photos.
*   **Mitigation:** Inject the **Negative Constraint Stack**: `waxy skin, face-filter smoothing, digital smoothing, plastic texture, over-retouched, CGI render` [25, 95]. Simultaneously force the physical parameters in the positive prompt: `micro-pore skin texture with natural oil variations, visible fine lines, and realistic vellus hair (peach fuzz) along the cheeks and jawline catching the light` [25, 95, 194].

#### 7.1.2 The "Floating Product" (Missing Contact Shadows)
*   **The Flaw:** Products look like they are floating in space or poorly photoshopped onto a background because the model failed to calculate ambient occlusion.
*   **Mitigation:** Specify the surface contact physically: `The product is resting firmly on a solid travertine pedestal, casting a dark, sharp, high-density contact shadow directly beneath its base where it touches the stone. Include ambient occlusion where the product meets the surface` [7, 120].

#### 7.1.3 "Helmet Hair" (Smudged Hairlines & Fused Cards)
*   **The Flaw:** Hair is rendered as a single, uniform plastic block or smudged into the skin at the hairline, looking like a low-polygon video game asset.
*   **Mitigation:** Direct the AI to use the Marschner hair model: `Specify anisotropic highlights with hair rendered as individual, distinct strands and natural flyaways. Primary highlight (un-tinted, tight) shifted toward the tip, secondary highlight (warm-tinted, broad) shifted toward the root. Hairline must show realistic scalp transition with individual hair follicles emerging naturally` [25, 87, 88].

#### 7.1.4 "Melted Packaging & Warped Typography"
*   **The Flaw:** Text labels or packaging outlines warp, stretch, or default to unreadable, alien-looking gibberish characters.
*   **Mitigation:** Enable **Thinking Mode** / **Dynamic Reasoning** on the prompt orchestrator. Apply the **Text Distance Rule**: put literal text in quotes and map its spatial layout precisely: `A rectangular white paper label, centered on the front face of the glass bottle, with exact text 'PURE OXYGEN' printed in a bold, black sans-serif font. The text is perfectly aligned horizontally, with a 2cm margin of white space on all sides of the label` [11, 94].

# 9. Psychological Science vs. Marketing Claims

When designing corporate headshots and commercial visuals, the NEROZARB system draws a strict, scientifically audited line between **proven cognitive psychological evidence** and typical **"pop psychology" marketing claims**. 

### 9.1 Audited Visual Psychology Framework

#### 9.1.1 The "Catchlight Sparkle" & Human Attentiveness
*   **Marketing Pop Claim:** "Catchlights in the eyes make people buy from you instantly because of subconscious hypnosis." (Unproven commercial hyperbole).
*   **Empirical Visual Science:** Cognitive neuroscience confirms that human facial recognition systems are heavily weighted toward the eyes. The presence of a **catchlight** (a sharp, specular reflection of a light source in the cornea) is a visual signal that the eye is moist, curved, and active. Psychologically, viewers register this moist catchlight as a sign of **attentiveness, vibrancy, and cognitive engagement** [13, 21]. Conversely, a pupil without a catchlight is a "dead eye," which is registered by the brain as flat, listless, or untrustworthy, mimicking the appearance of illness or a mannequin [22].
*   **NEROZARB Standard:** Every human portrait must feature a sharp, high-intensity silver catchlight positioned in the upper-right (or upper-left) quadrant of the iris. [77, 192]

#### 9.1.2 The "Duchenne Smile" & Human Trust
*   **Marketing Pop Claim:** "A big, toothy grin in your headshot makes you look like a successful, charismatic millionaire." (Often backfires, as forced smiles look manipulative or unserious in corporate environments).
*   **Empirical Cognitive Science:** Psychological studies on microexpressions demonstrate that humans can subconsciously distinguish a forced, polite smile from a genuine **Duchenne smile** within milliseconds. A Duchenne smile is characterized by the involuntary contraction of the *orbicularis oculi* muscles around the eyes, creating "crow's feet" and a slight lifting of the cheeks (often called "smizing" or "smiling with the eyes") [292]. This authentic smile stimulates the release of neurotransmitters like dopamine in the viewer, signaling **genuine friendliness, approachability, and safety**, which forms a foundation for mutual trust [292, 295].
*   **NEROZARB Standard:** Direct the subject (or prompt the AI) for a "Duchenne smile with gentle contraction around the outer eye muscles and a relaxed, naturally closed mouth." [25, 292, 295]

#### 9.1.3 Background Color and Cognitive Focus
*   **Marketing Pop Claim:** "Blue backgrounds make you look smart, while red backdrops make you close deals faster." (Over-generalized, culturally dependent pop claim).
*   **Empirical Visual Science:** Contrast and visual isolation dictate cognitive focus. A plain, desaturated gray background (e.g., #EFECE1 or #B0C4DE) has a neutral psychological response, ensuring the viewer's visual processing power is allocated entirely to the subject's face [284]. A highly saturated backdrop causes visual fatigue, as the viewer's eyes must constantly adjust between the high-frequency colored background and the low-frequency face [305].
*   **NEROZARB Standard:** Use plain, desaturated gray or warm ivory backdrops (#EFECE1) for executive headshots to maximize facial focus and eliminate cognitive clutter [284].

# 12. Evidence Gaps & Quality Assurance Gaps

While 2026-era computational imaging represents a massive leap forward, several technical gaps remain unresolved. The NEROZARB QA Team must monitor these areas manually:
1.  **Screen-Space SSS Outline Artifacts:** In screen-space Subsurface Scattering models (such as those in Unreal Engine or real-time raster models), a distinct gray outline or halo often appears at the boundary where a non-SSS material (like a metal ring or garment) occludes a SSS material (like skin or wax) [164]. This must be cleaned up in post-production.
2.  **High-Frequency Weave Smearing:** In high-speed video generation (e.g., 8K outputs from Kling or Runway), fast camera pans can cause the AI to "smear" or lose high-frequency weave data (such as the fine diagonal lines of twill or the diamond pattern of birdseye), leading to temporary patches of flat color [22].
3.  **Extreme Contrast Banding:** Under high-contrast, low-key lighting (such as split or chiaroscuro lighting with a 10:1 ratio), generative models can produce visible banding artifacts in the shadow transition areas (the penumbra) instead of a smooth, analog-like gradient [164].
4.  **Temporal Texture Shimmer:** When animating garments in video models, fine patterns like pinstripes or herringbone can exhibit a "shimmering" or "moiré" effect between frames due to a lack of frame-to-frame pixel-level temporal coherence [21].
